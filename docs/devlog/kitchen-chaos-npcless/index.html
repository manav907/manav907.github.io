How Three Physics Problems Created One Nearly Invisible Bug

A Broken Game and an Unfamiliar Project

When I first joined Hopscotch Games as a QA tester, one of the projects I was introduced to was Kitchen Chaos, a Roblox game I was asked to test.

My first experience was confusing. I couldn't figure out how to progress through the game. I wasn't sure whether I was misunderstanding the mechanics, missing an interaction, or encountering a bug.

After asking around, I learned that the game had been stuck in a broken state for about a month. The progression issue was already known, but it hadn't been resolved.

Since I was new to the team, I was initially assigned simpler QA tasks: checking UI consistency, verifying that text matched its corresponding images, and making sure interface elements were presented correctly.

Still, the broken progression kept bothering me.

I started investigating it on the side while continuing my assigned testing. I wasn't particularly familiar with the project's systems, and I didn't have a clear starting point.

Over time, however, I began noticing a pattern in how the game was failing.

Two Symptoms, No Clear Explanation

The progression issue seemed to involve NPCs that weren't reliably present where they were supposed to be.

Sometimes, I would reach an area and find no NPCs at all. This made me suspect they hadn't spawned correctly.

Other times, an NPC would be visible until I approached it. Getting close enough to make physical contact could cause it to disappear almost instantly.

That suggested a different possibility. Perhaps approaching an NPC triggered an interaction script that accidentally destroyed or disabled it.

One symptom looked like a spawning problem. The other looked like an interaction problem.

I couldn't tell whether these were separate bugs or different symptoms of the same underlying issue.

I eventually asked a developer about the NPCs, hoping to rule out some straightforward explanations.

Were they being destroyed? Were they disabled? Could they be spawning at incorrect coordinates?

According to the developer, the NPCs weren't being destroyed or disabled, and their coordinates were correct.

That left me with an apparent contradiction.

The NPCs were clearly missing from the player's perspective, yet the information available suggested that they still existed and were positioned correctly.

I didn't have enough evidence to challenge those findings, but they didn't explain what I was seeing either.

I was examining the state left behind by the bug without knowing what happened during the disappearance.

An Accidental Advantage: Recording at 240 FPS

I happened to have a setup that turned out to be unusually useful.

I generally played Roblox with an uncapped frame rate rather than its default limit. This wasn't something I'd configured specifically for QA; it was simply how I preferred to play.

Separately, I'd been experimenting with recording gameplay at 240 FPS because I wanted to capture slow-motion footage from another game as a hobby.

Neither choice was originally related to Kitchen Chaos.

But after repeatedly seeing NPCs vanish without an obvious explanation, I realized the recording setup might help me catch something I was missing.

Having the setup was coincidental. Deciding to use it for debugging was deliberate.

I recorded the glitch and began scrubbing through the footage frame by frame, concentrating on the moments immediately before an NPC disappeared.

Instead of repeatedly observing the aftermath, I could finally examine the transition itself.

That's when I noticed something I hadn't been able to see during normal gameplay.

The One Frame That Changed the Investigation

The NPC wasn't being destroyed. It was falling through the floor.

The critical transition was visible in just one captured frame.

An NPC that appeared to be standing normally on the walking surface suddenly passed below it and disappeared from view.

At normal playback speed, the movement was practically imperceptible. The character seemed to vanish instantly, leaving almost no indication of what had happened.

The footage didn't establish that the entire physics failure lasted exactly one simulation frame. It showed that the decisive fall-through behavior was observable in only one frame of the recording.

That distinction didn't change the significance of the discovery.

I finally had direct evidence of the mechanism behind the disappearance.

It also connected the two symptoms I'd been struggling to explain.

If an NPC fell through the floor sometime after spawning, before the player encountered it, the result looked like a spawning failure.

If the NPC remained standing until a player approached and physically pushed it, the same fall-through behavior could look like a broken interaction.

What appeared to be two unrelated bugs was actually the same physical failure occurring under different circumstances.

The recording shifted the investigation away from spawning and interaction scripts.

The question was no longer why the NPCs were supposedly being deleted.

It was why they could pass through a floor that should have supported them.

From Visual Evidence to a Physics Explanation

I shared the footage and my findings with a developer.

Until then, most of my investigation had involved observing gameplay, questioning the apparent symptoms, and examining recorded footage.

I had established what was happening, but I didn't yet understand why the physics allowed it to happen.

The conversation helped bridge that gap.

With evidence of the NPCs falling through the floor, we could focus on the physical setup rather than continuing to investigate spawning or interaction behavior.

Three configuration problems emerged from that discussion.

1. Incorrect Normals and an Improper Walking Surface

The first issue involved the floor's normals and walking-surface configuration.

Surface normals describe the orientation of geometry. Although visual normals alone don't necessarily control physical collisions, the incorrectly configured surface geometry was part of the collision problem identified during the discussion.

The floor appeared to be an ordinary walking surface, but its setup wasn't providing the reliable physical contact the NPCs needed.

This was a useful distinction. The environment looked correct from the player's perspective, so there had been little reason to suspect the floor while investigating missing characters.

2. Complex Mesh Colliders on NPC Feet

The second problem involved the NPCs' collision geometry.

Instead of simpler capsule or cylinder-shaped colliders, the NPCs used complex mesh colliders around their feet.

Mesh-based collision shapes can produce complicated contact behavior because their geometry is more irregular.

For NPCs that primarily need predictable contact with walking surfaces, this complexity wasn't particularly useful.

The developer's explanation helped connect this configuration to the behavior I'd recorded.

When a player approached closely enough to physically push an NPC, the additional contact could disturb an already unreliable interaction between the NPC's feet and the floor.

What looked like an interaction bug was actually a physical disturbance exposing a collision problem.

3. Gravity That the NPCs Didn't Need

The third problem was gravity.

The NPCs were configured to be affected by gravity even though their intended behavior didn't require it.

Gravity wasn't necessarily responsible for the initial loss of contact with the floor. Its role became important afterward.

Once an NPC lost support and began passing through the walking surface, gravity could continue pulling it downward, carrying it outside the playable area.

Together, the three problems explained the failure:

Improper walking surface → unstable collider contact → NPC falls through the floor → gravity carries it below the map.

The discussion also helped clarify why the earlier debugging information had been misleading.

The NPCs could remain in the correct object hierarchy even after physically leaving the playable area.

An object's place in the Roblox Studio hierarchy describes how it is organized, not necessarily where it is positioned in the 3D world.

Likewise, confirming coordinates at one moment doesn't prove an NPC remains there throughout gameplay.

This didn't establish exactly why the earlier coordinate check hadn't revealed the problem. But it explained why those checks weren't sufficient to rule out a physical displacement.

My footage established the fall-through behavior. The developer discussion connected that observation to the underlying physics configuration.

That collaboration turned an unexplained disappearance into a specific technical problem.

From Root Cause to an Actionable Fix

The investigation gave the developers a clearer direction for addressing the progression blocker.

The corrective work involved fixing the floor's walking-surface setup, replacing unsuitable NPC foot mesh colliders with simpler collision geometry, and removing unnecessary gravity.

Each correction addressed a different contributor to the failure rather than treating the NPC disappearance as a single unexplained scripting problem.

My contribution was identifying the fall-through behavior, capturing evidence, and bringing that discovery into a technical discussion that helped establish the root cause.

I wasn't responsible for implementing the corrections, and I don't have a documented record of the exact changes ultimately deployed.

The original symptoms also provided two useful regression scenarios.

The first involved observing whether NPCs remained on the floor over time without player contact. The second involved approaching and physically pushing NPCs to see whether the same displacement occurred.

Successful verification would require the NPCs to remain within the playable area under both conditions, with gameplay progression continuing normally.

The same high-frame-rate recording approach could also be used to compare the collision behavior before and after correction.

The documented outcome of my investigation was identifying the failure mechanism and helping narrow down its causes. A confirmed post-fix verification result would require additional evidence.

What I Took Away from the Investigation

The most important lesson wasn't simply that several physics configuration problems could combine to break a game.

It was learning to question what the available debugging evidence actually proves.

The developer's initial checks weren't necessarily incorrect. They simply didn't capture the physical event responsible for the NPCs disappearing.

I also learned that identifying a bug doesn't always require independently understanding every system behind it.

My frame-by-frame analysis revealed the missing piece of evidence. Discussing that evidence with someone more familiar with the game's physics helped turn it into an actionable explanation.

The recording setup had been available by coincidence, but recognizing its usefulness and applying it to the investigation was a deliberate decision.

In Kitchen Chaos, a month-old progression blocker had multiple misleading symptoms, but the decisive evidence was visible in a single captured frame.

Finding that frame was what turned a confusing disappearance into an actionable physics investigation.
